The .alm files

Every dignity score in Solar Fire comes from a definition file of type .alm, edited in Utilities > Dignity/Almuten Editor. Knowing which file feeds which table is the key to changing anything.

  • essdig.alm drives the Essential Dignities table, the Horary report's dignity columns and mutual receptions. One definition.
  • house.alm drives house-cusp almutens. One definition.
  • general.alm drives the Dignity/Almuten page object and Almuten Scores tabulation. It holds several definitions, each one a different almuten calculation; the page object needs three data lines per definition (name, ranked planets, scores).
  • hyleg.alm (with the aspect set hyleg.asp) drives the hyleg calculations.

essdig, house and hyleg must stay in Solar Fire's program folder. Your own definition files go in the Almutens & Dignities folder of Solar Fire User Files, and a page object can be pointed at one of them instead of the default. To try another scheme without losing the original, open the file, use File > Save As, and edit the copy.

Changing the triplicity scheme (for example to Dorothean): open the Dignity/Almuten Editor, open essdig.alm, click Options, choose the triplicity type, OK, then File > Save. Do the same in house.alm so the house almutens match. Reopen the chart to see new scores.

A definition is either Degree Dignities (the dignities at one position: a planet's own score, or every planet's score at a cusp, which gives that cusp's almuten) or Almuten – Sum over Points (each planet's dignities summed over several positions; the top scorer is the almuten). It can also be limited to day or night charts, so you can keep separate day and night definitions.

The Options button sets the rulership set, triplicity and term type, whether signs start at 0° or 1° (which only shifts the terms), the house system and cusp offsets used for house scoring, and the Part of Fortune formula for almutens.

Almutens

An almuten is the planet with the most dignity at a place, or over a set of places. Solar Fire computes three kinds.

  • Degree almuten of one position: add each planet's dignities there. The manual's example: at 28° Libra by day, Venus rules, Saturn is exalted and the day triplicity ruler, Mars has the term and Jupiter the face, so Saturn scores highest and is the almuten of that degree.
  • House almutens: the degree almuten of each cusp (from house.alm). Read the almuten of a house as its strongest significator, alongside its sign ruler. Where they differ, the almuten shows who really has authority over that house's affairs.
  • Almuten of the chart (Almuten Scores, from general.alm): sums each planet's dignities at several key places. Ibn Ezra's version uses the Sun, Moon, Ascendant, Part of Fortune and prenatal syzygy. Some versions add planet of the day and hour and house position. The winner is read as the ruling planet of the nativity, the significator of the person's character.

To build your own: in the editor add a definition, choose Almuten – Sum over Points, click Selected Points, and add the points to sum (chart points, cusps, prenatal syzygy positions, Arabic parts). Each point can use its own position or that of its dispositor, triplicity ruler, term ruler or face ruler. For a part with day and night formulas, add both; Solar Fire uses the one that fits the chart.

Minimum Honors stops a planet becoming almuten unless it has at least that many dignities (honours) there; at present only hyleg.alm uses it.

Hylegs

The hyleg (apheta, giver of life) is the point that stands for the native's vitality, used in length-of-life and health work. Solar Fire computes three methods, each with its own rules. Edit hyleg.alm and hyleg.asp to change scores, orbs and offsets.

The methods often disagree; compare them, check each candidate's condition, and use the one whose rules you follow. The prenatal syzygy used here can be cast as a chart from Chart > Lunar Phase.

Sect, receptions and horary

  • Planetary Sects: Cht is the chart's sect (Sun above or below the horizon), Plc whether the planet is on the Sun's side of the horizon, Sgn whether its sign is masculine (diurnal) or feminine (nocturnal). A planet whose own sect matches all three is in hayz, a strong accidental dignity; one contrary on all three is ex conditione.
  • Mutual Receptions lists receptions in order rulership, exaltation, triplicity, term, face. In the Essential Dignities table an m marks a reception for that dignity, + the planet's own dignity and − its detriment or fall.
  • Planetary Hour/Day uses unequal hours: day and night each divided into twelve. The hour ruler agreeing with the querent's significator is a traditional test of a horary's radicality.
  • Horary Considerations are the classical cautions before judgement (strictures): void Moon, via combusta, Ascendant too early or late, Saturn in the 7th, and the Moon's speed. "None" means none apply.

Time lords

  • Firdaria: planetary periods of fixed length (the Sun's 10 years, Venus 8, Mercury 13, Moon 9, Saturn 11, Jupiter 12, Mars 7, then the nodes) starting from the Sun by day or the Moon by night, each split into seven sub-periods. Read the period lord's natal condition and its relation to the sub-lord. The variant changes where the nodes fall for night charts.
  • Direction of Divisions (term directions): the Ascendant directed through the terms by primary motion; each term ruler governs a stretch of life. Solar Fire uses Placidian (mundane) directions with your chosen rate key; the term type comes from the first definition of the .alm file the object uses.
  • Zodiacal Releasing: sign-based periods released from the Lot of Spirit (career, action) or Fortune (body, circumstances). Level 1 gives chapters of years; click a date to drill down to levels 2–4. Loosing of the bond (LOB) marks a turning point within a period.
  • Annual profection: one sign per year from the Ascendant; the ruler of the profected sign is lord of the year, and transits and returns to it are emphasised.

The default exaltation column adds Uranus exalted in Scorpio and in fall in Taurus, which traditional astrology doesn't use. If it turns up in your scores, edit the Old set in the Rulerships and Weightings Editor to leave Scorpio and Taurus with only the Moon's exaltation and fall.

Weightings (same editor) set how much each point counts in element, mode and ray balances: 3 for Sun, Moon, Ascendant and MC, 2 for Mercury, Venus and Mars, 1 for Jupiter to Pluto, 0 for everything else. They don't affect dignity scores.

Reading the scores

  • The score is a summary, not the judgement. +5 in domicile and +5 from triplicity plus term plus face are different conditions: the first is a planet at home with full authority; the second a guest with several minor claims. Read the columns, not just the total.
  • Peregrine (p) means no essential dignity at all: the planet acts without resources of its own and depends on its dispositor and on reception. A peregrine planet in reception is far better off than one without.
  • Detriment and fall describe a planet acting against its nature or out of favour, not simply "bad". Look at its dispositor's condition, and at receptions, which can repair it.
  • Combine essential with accidental dignity. House (angular, succedent, cadent), speed, direction, combustion and sect are not in the essential score; the Rulerships report and Planetary Sects object cover some of them.
  • Follow dispositorship. The final dispositor (Rulerships report) and the almuten of the chart point to the planets that carry the whole nativity.
  • Check the scheme. If a score surprises you, look at which triplicity and term table produced it: a planet may have a term by Egyptian bounds and none by Ptolemaic.
