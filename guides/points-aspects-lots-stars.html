Point files

Solar Fire separates which points appear from which points get aspects and from which points dynamic reports use, each in its own named file. That lets you show the angles without drawing aspects to them, or run transits from the outer planets only.

  • Displayed Points is the main switch: the standard 36 points (planets, nodes, angles, Vertex, Fortune, main asteroids, Chiron, Black Moon and the transneptunians). Edit a copy: in the Chart Point Selection dialog, click a point to move it between Selected and Unselected; the Quick Select frame switches whole groups (Planets, Asteroids, TransNeps, Others) on or off.
  • Aspected Points can be smaller than Displayed Points. A common traditional choice: aspect lines between the seven planets only, with the angles, nodes and Fortune displayed but not aspected.
  • User-Defined Points put up to 50 extra points into the main ring: midpoints, fixed degrees, stars, parts, asteroids, extra bodies. Choose None.ptu to remove them. Extra Ring Points take the same kinds of point plus prior lunations, eclipses and planetary nodes, in a ring of their own; only wheel styles built with an extra ring show it.
  • Dynamic point files are chosen in the Dynamic Report Selection dialog (Module 9): Radix, Transiting and Progressing points, plus Extra points for each. Keep extra sets to about 50 points or the report becomes unreadable.

Calculated points

  • Part of Fortune. Different Day/Night uses Ascendant + Moon − Sun by day and Ascendant + Sun − Moon by night, the ancient rule. Fixed Formula uses the day formula always, as older software did. This setting governs wheels and reports; the Arabic Parts report follows its own file instead.
  • Lunar node. True node is the actual node; mean node smooths out the wobble. Traditional texts were computed with the mean node.
  • Black Moon. The lunar apogee, true or mean. It isn't the asteroid Lilith or the hypothetical Dark Moon; the FAQ "How do I display all three Liliths?" explains adding those.

Aspect sets and orbs

An aspect set holds every aspect Solar Fire knows, each switched on (+) or off, with its orbs. Edit a copy from Chart Options > Aspect Set, then Edit. Double-click an aspect, or use Restore and Remove, to switch it.

Orbs

  • Natal orbs apply to charts and reports other than progressions and dynamic reports. Progressed orbs apply to progressed charts. Transit orbs set when a dynamic report says a contact is entering or leaving.
  • Each can differ for luminaries (when the Sun or Moon is one of the pair) and other points, and for applying and separating.
  • Applying and separating are worked out from both planets' motion; an angle (Ascendant, MC, Vertex, Equatorial Ascendant) counts as stationary. Between two charts the second chart is held still: the inner wheel of a biwheel, the chart across a synastry grid, the first chart in a synastry report.

A traditional aspect set

  1. Copy an aspect set (Chart Options > Aspect Set, select one, Save As) and name it, say, "Ptolemaic moieties".
  2. Switch off everything but conjunction, sextile, square, trine and opposition.
  3. Click Advanced, choose Use Planet Orb Ratios, tick Use Moieties and Use Sign as well as Orb.
  4. Set the base orb for each aspect and give each planet a ratio that yields its orb. Lilly's orbs (whole orbs, before halving) are about: Sun 17°, Moon 12½°, Saturn 10°, Jupiter 12°, Mars 7½°, Venus 7–8°, Mercury 7°.
  5. Save, select the set, and check a chart: the Sun and Saturn should then be in aspect within 13½° (8½ + 5).

For Hellenistic work make a second set with Use Aspects by Sign Only and the five Ptolemaic aspects.

Arabic parts

A part (lot) is a point found as A + B − C: the distance from C to B, measured from A. Solar Fire's part files are edited in the Arabic Parts Editor (Chart Options > Files > Arabic Parts, select a file, Edit).

  • Supplied files: Allparts.arp (all the NOVA parts) and Moreparts.arp (150 parts). Copy one and cut it down to the lots you use, so the report stays readable.
  • Each part has a full name (keep it under about 30 characters), a unique abbreviation (up to 15) that other parts can refer to, a Day, Night or Both setting, and the formula. A reversing part such as Fortune or Spirit needs two entries, one Day and one Night.
  • Formula elements can be a chart point, a fixed degree (e.g. 15Cn00), the dispositor of a point, a house cusp, the ruler of a house cusp, another part in the file (e.g. FortD), or a prenatal lunation.
  • Reordering or deleting parts breaks any almuten definition that uses that file; re-select the parts in the Dignity/Almuten Editor afterwards.

Reading lots. Treat a lot like a house cusp for its topic: its sign ruler (and that ruler's condition and house) shows how the matter fares; planets conjunct or aspecting the lot colour it. Fortune concerns the body and circumstances, Spirit the mind and actions; the other Hermetic lots (Eros, Necessity, Courage, Victory, Nemesis) are built from them.

Fixed stars

  • Star files: BRADY.FST, 50 stars with Bernadette Brady's interpretations and +/− markers for especially easy or difficult stars; PTOLEMY.FST, Ptolemy's stars with their planetary natures; ALLSTARS.FST, about 290 stars without text. Choose one from Chart Options > Files > Fixed Stars.
  • Star Aspects lists chart points in aspect to stars by ecliptic longitude. Use a small orb (1° or less) and mostly conjunctions. A star far from the ecliptic can be "conjunct" by longitude while sitting nowhere near the planet in the sky; the 3D option tests the real distance.
  • Star Parans lists stars that reach an angle at the same moment as a chart point at the birth latitude, whatever their longitude. Brady works this way and suggests an orb of 15–20 minutes. Choose which of rise, set, culminate and anti-culminate to include from Reports > Parans to Use.
  • Editing stars: the Fixed Star Editor adds stars or other objects, changes the epoch, sorts the file and shows a star in the planetarium.

Parans

A paran (from paranatellonta, rising together) happens when two bodies are on the angles at the same time: one rising as another culminates, both setting together, and so on. It depends on latitude, not on longitude, so it links stars and planets that ecliptic aspects can't. Solar Fire shows parans in three places: the Star Parans report (stars with chart points), the speculum's paran tables and Completed Parans (planets with each other within 1°), and Solar Maps' paran lines (Module 18), which show the latitudes where each pair is in paran.

Paran comparison lists parans between two charts, a mundane form of synastry. The same coordinates can be shown as wheels with the zodiacal analogue charts on Chart > Harmonic/Transform (see the Subsidiary Charts Guide).
