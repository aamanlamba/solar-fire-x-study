The report screen

  • Modulus (point sort and midpoint reports): pick a harmonic 1–12 or type any angle, e.g. 45 for the 8th harmonic.
  • Orb (midpoint axes and trees, star parans): 0–10°, e.g. 0 20 for 20′.
  • Use Glyphs, Use Colors, Bold Text, Full Headers: display switches; Full Headers off saves paper.
  • Charts: switch to another calculated chart; tick Auto-apply to scroll through charts and watch the report update.
  • Copy puts plain text on the clipboard (abbreviations replace glyphs; columns may need tidying). Print prints with glyphs and colours.

Primary directions

A primary direction asks how far the sky must turn after birth before one point (the promissor) reaches the place another (the significator) held at birth; each degree of that turning is roughly a year of life. Choose a primary type under Direction type and select Directions to Radix.

ChoiceWhat it meansPick it when
Primary MundaneDirects the bodies' actual positions, latitude included ("with latitude"); aspects are mundane, measured by position in the diurnal arcYou follow Placidus, or want mundane aspects
Primary ZodiacalDirects the ecliptic degrees, latitude dropped ("without latitude"); aspects are by longitude, cast from the promissorYou follow the medieval and Renaissance zodiacal practice
Primary van DamWim van Dam's Placidian variant with its own time measure, positions given in longitudeStudying van Dam's "primary event analysis"
Placidus methodMundane position as a proportion of the semi-arc (Ptolemy, Placidus)Ptolemaic and Placidian directions
Regiomontanus methodMundane position from the circle of position through the north and south points of the horizonRenaissance and 17th-century directions (Regiomontanus, Morin, Lilly)
Classical converseConverse of A to B is the direct direction of B to A: the same arcPtolemaic practice; Solar Fire's default
Modern converseDirects A backwards to B, giving a different arc19th-century and later practice

In the report, Placidus mundane positions are shown as 0–360°, a quarter for each quadrant: rising at 0°, IC 90°, setting 180°, MC 270°. So a body directed to the MC shows 270°. Transits to Directions is also available: it finds when a transiting planet crosses the zodiacal degree a directed point has reached (its directional place).

Reading a report line

A line reads, for example: Ura (10) Sqr Ven (7) (X) (Tr-Na) Jun 12 1993 11:07:23pm BST 19Cp23 R 19Li23 D: transiting Uranus in the natal 10th, retrograde at 19° Capricorn, exactly squares natal Venus in the 7th.

For primary directions the first point is the promissor and the second the significator; the house shown is that of the directed point.

Time Map

From a dynamic report, Time Map draws each contact as a bar across the report period, grouping entering, exact and leaving into one line. Subscripts show the kind of point: t transiting, p progressed, d directed, n natal, r radix. Hit Nos labels repeated transit passes (1:3, 2:3, 3:3); Date shows the date of each exact hit (or the time, for periods under a week). Click lines to select, then Delete; drag to reorder; double-click for the interpretation.

Recipes

  • Year-ahead transits: natal radix, start 1 January, period 1 year, Transits to Radix, Jupiter to Pluto as transiting points, Entering/Leaving on, plus Stations and Eclipses.
  • Primary directions for a life: Directions to Radix, Primary Zodiacal (or Mundane), Regiomontanus (or Placidus), period 80 years, the seven planets and angles as progressing and radix points, conjunction and opposition first, then add the other Ptolemaic aspects. Run it again with Converse ticked and Merge on.
  • Solar arc with Ascendant arc: run Solar Arc Directions to Radix, then run Ascendant Arc with Merge ticked.
  • Profections: Directions to Radix with Annual profections; look for the profected Ascendant reaching natal planets.
  • A relationship: one partner as Dynamic Radix, the other as Alternate Radix Positions, Progressions to Radix.
