Planet/angle lines

These are the core astrocartography lines: where on Earth, at the birth moment, each body was on one of the four angles. Solar Maps draws them two ways and you choose one at a time in Select Lines.

  • In Mundo uses right ascension and declination: the body actually rising, culminating, setting or anti-culminating. This is Jim Lewis's Astro*Carto*Graphy.
  • Zodiacal uses ecliptic longitude: the body's degree is on the Ascendant, MC and so on of a chart relocated there. It matches what you see in a relocated wheel.

The MC and IC lines are the same either way and run north–south. The rising and setting lines differ for bodies far from the ecliptic (Pluto, many asteroids, most stars), and curve on flat map projections because they're great circles. In mundo, midpoints and aspect lines use right ascension; zodiacal ones use longitude.

Reading angle lines

Read a line as the planet made angular, and so prominent, in that place. The angle says which part of life it works through; the planet's nature and natal condition say how. A well-dignified benefic on an angle is a different proposition from an afflicted malefic there.

  • Crossings. Where two lines cross, both planets are angular together; the paran from that crossing carries their combination along the whole latitude.
  • Remote activation. A place can work through dealings with it (clients, family, travel there) without living there.
  • The natal chart still governs. A line can't give what the nativity doesn't promise. It shows where a natal theme is brought forward.
  • Check with the wheel. Cast the relocated chart (Solar Fire Chart > Locality, or the airplane tool in Solar Maps) to see houses and rulers as well.

Paran lines

A paran line marks a latitude where two bodies are on angles at the same moment, for example the Sun culminating as Saturn sets. Unlike angle lines, a paran is felt along the whole latitude band, not at one longitude, which is why Map Parans lists cities by latitude. Solar Maps can show parans of one chosen point with every other, or all of them (too many for a world map). Stars need a star file open first, and a paran point needn't be a displayed point. Work with an orb of about a degree of latitude, and set Limit of Lat (60° is typical) to drop polar parans.

Local space

Local space (Michael Erlewine) draws lines from the chart's place in the direction each planet lay at birth, by azimuth. Follow a line from where you are and you're said to meet that planet's people and matters. The same directions work at the scale of a city or a house: print the Local Horizon Chart onto a transparency, centre it on the place, align north, and read the lines across the map. On a world map the lines are great circles, so they curve on flat projections but are straight on the globe view. City Directions lists the places each line passes through.

Celestial and geodetic

  • Celestial projection (the normal one) shows the true sky over the Earth: the ecliptic line and the sign boundaries where each sign is overhead.
  • Geodetic projection lays the zodiac along the equator as bands of longitude, 0° Aries at Greenwich by default (you can set another start, east or west, in Options > Geodetic Zodiac). The title bar then says "(Geodetic)". Each place has fixed geodetic angles (the manual gives Washington DC as MC 14° Capricorn 57′, Ascendant 23° Libra 09′), so planets in those degrees are angular there whatever the birth time. Used by Sepharial and Johndro for mundane work, and useful for untimed charts.
  • Eclipse paths show where a solar eclipse is total and where it is partial; lunar eclipses are seen from a whole hemisphere and aren't mapped. An eclipse is usually read as strongest under the path of totality.

Lines over time

Chart > Subsidiary Chart (available once a natal chart is open) draws transiting, progressed or directed planets while keeping the natal angles, so you see how far each planet has moved relative to your own map. That's different from casting a transit chart, whose angles belong to the transit moment. The options are transits, secondary progressions, tertiary progressions, solar arc directions, and Sec.Prog + Transits (CCG), Jim Lewis's Cyclo*Carto*Graphy: progressed Sun, Moon, Mercury, Venus and Mars with transiting Jupiter to Pluto. Restore Natal Chart goes back. Transiting lines sweep the globe quickly (the Sun about once a year); progressed lines move a degree or so a year.

Reports

  • Map Parans: parans and cities in one list from north to south, limited to the map shown and the latitude limit. A city within a fraction of a degree of a paran latitude lives under that paran.
  • Local Parans: only parans within 2° of the chart's latitude, the strongest ones where the chart is cast (or relocated).
  • City Directions: cities on (conjunct) or opposite each local space line, with orb, from the map's city file.
  • Relocation Interpretations: choose a city and Solar Maps writes a report covering planets on angles, local space and parans, opened in your word processor. Save it under a new name, as the file is overwritten next time.

A working method

  1. Open the natal chart in Solar Maps and show only the seven planets (and the nodes, if you use them) as displayed points.
  2. Turn on In Mundo angle lines only. Note which planets run near the places that matter: birthplace, where you live, where you're thinking of going.
  3. Judge each nearby planet by its natal condition (sign dignity, sect, house rulership) before calling a line good or bad.
  4. Add parans for the most important planet (Parans Only Of…), then run Local Parans for each place of interest.
  5. Cast the relocated wheel for each place to see which houses the planets fall in and who rules the new angles.
  6. Add local space lines last, for directions from where you live.
  7. For timing, add Sec.Prog + Transits (CCG) or transits for the current date.
